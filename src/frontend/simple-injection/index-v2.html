Research purpose

Why forecast Injection behavior with a surrogate?

A surrogate trained on Moldex3D DOE results screens Sprue Pressure curves and Filling Pressure distributions across geometry and process settings. It narrows the candidates that deserve deeper review before another full simulation cycle.

ProblemRepeating a full analysis for every DOE combination slows design review.
TargetCompare how geometry and process settings change pressure curves and filling distributions.
ScopeScreen within the trained DOE and fixed-gate domain, then inspect the driving Features with XAI.
How the outputs are used

The Sprue Pressure curve describes inlet pressure over time, while the Filling Pressure distribution compares pressure ratios across filled regions. Review both with XAI when comparing process candidates.

Sprue Pressure

Pressure Curve

How to read it

The x-axis is time (s), and the y-axis is pressure at the sprue inlet (MPa). The peak is the maximum sprue pressure required by the process; sharp changes can indicate filling or packing transitions and changes in flow resistance.

This is a surrogate prediction. Compare it with Moldex3D or experimental results before making final mold or machine decisions.

Distribution

-
How to read it

G1 through G10 run from lower to higher pressure ranges. Each bar shows the percentage of part volume within that pressure range.

More volume in higher-numbered groups means a larger portion of the part requires high filling pressure. This chart summarizes the distribution, not spatial location.
